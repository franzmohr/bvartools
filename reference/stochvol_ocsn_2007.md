# Stochastic Volatility

Produces a draw of log-volatilities based on Omori, Chib, Shephard and
Nakajima (2007).

## Usage

``` r
stochvol_ocsn_2007(y, h, sigma, h_init, constant)
```

## Arguments

- y:

  a \\T \times K\\ matrix containing the time series.

- h:

  a \\T \times K\\ matrix of the current draw of log-volatilities.

- sigma:

  a \\K \times 1\\ vector of variances of log-volatilities, where the
  \\i\\th element corresponds to the \\i\\th column in `y`. Must be
  finite and positive.

- h_init:

  a \\K \times 1\\ vector of the initial states of log-volatilities,
  where the \\i\\th element corresponds to the \\i\\th column in `y`.

- constant:

  a \\K \times 1\\ vector of constants that should be added to \\y^2\\
  before taking the natural logarithm. The \\i\\th element corresponds
  to the \\i\\th column in `y`. Must be finite and positive. See
  'Details'.

## Value

A \\T \times K\\ matrix of log-volatility draws.

## Details

For each column in `y` the function produces a posterior draw of the
log-volatility \\h\\ for the model \$\$y\_{t} = e^{\frac{1}{2}h_t}
\epsilon\_{t},\$\$ where \\\epsilon_t \sim N(0, 1)\\ and \\h_t\\ is
assumed to evolve according to a random walk \$\$h_t = h\_{t - 1} +
u_t,\$\$ with \\u_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2)\\.

The implementation follows the algorithm of Omori, Chib, Shephard and
Nakajima (2007) and performs the following steps:

1.  Perform the transformation \\y_t^\* = ln(y_t^2 + constant)\\.

2.  Obtain a sample from the ten-component normal mixture for
    approximating the log-\\\chi_1^2\\ distribution.

3.  Obtain a draw of log-volatilities.

The ten components approximate the log-\\\chi_1^2\\ distribution more
closely than the seven of
[`stochvol_ksc_1998`](https://franzmohr.github.io/bvartools/reference/stochvol_ksc_1998.md),
at a proportional cost in the first step.

The posterior precision of the log-volatility is tridiagonal – the
random walk contributes the two bands of \\D^\prime D\\ and the mixture
only a diagonal – so the draw is obtained from a banded Cholesky
factorisation, which costs \\O(T)\\ rather than the \\O(T^3)\\ of a
dense one.

The probabilities of the mixture components in step 2 are obtained in
logs and shifted by their period-wise maximum before they are
exponentiated. Weighting the component densities directly underflows to
zero for all ten components once an observation lies far enough out in
their tails, which leaves the probabilities of that period undefined and
selects a component that does not exist.

The arguments are checked before the first draw and a violation raises
an error. `sigma` and `constant` have to be finite and positive, since
the algorithm divides by the former and takes the logarithm of a sum
containing the latter, and all three of `sigma`, `h_init` and `constant`
have to have one element per column of `y`.

The draw uses R's random number generator, so
[`set.seed`](https://rdrr.io/r/base/Random.html) makes it reproducible.
